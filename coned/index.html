Continuing Education Guidelines for Permanent Deacons

1.0General

The National Directory for the Formation, Ministry, and Life of Permanent Deacons in the United States of America (USCCB, 2021) mandates ongoing continuing education and formation for deacons. See Norms below. A requirement for such education and formation is to be established by each diocese with the approval of the bishop.

National Norms
  • A program is to be planned for the first five years of their ministry and coordinated by the Director of the Permanent Diaconate. Deacons in their initial pastoral assignments should be carefully supervised by an exemplary designated pastor or priest supervisor especially appointed to this task by the diocesan bishop. (263)
  • Each diocesan Church is to establish a basic minimum of continuing education hours to be fulfilled on an annual basis by all diocesan deacons in active service. This would be in addition to time allocated for the annual diaconal community retreat. (267)
  • The goal of post-ordination formation is to responsibly address the various aspects of a deacon’s ministry, the development of his personality and, above all, his commitment to spiritual growth. Ongoing formation must include and harmonize all dimensions of the life and ministry of the deacon [human, spiritual, intellectual, and pastoral]. (251)

2.0Participation in Continuing Education and Formation

The Archdiocese of Mobile has established the following continuing education and formation guidelines for all deacons in active service.1 Specific guidelines pertain to newly ordained deacons for their first five years of ministry (2.1) and to experienced deacons who have served six or more years in ministry (2.2).

2.1Requirement for Newly Ordained

For their first five years after ordination, deacons will perform their ministry under the supervision of the Pastor of their assigned parish and the Director of Permanent Deacons. Newly ordained deacons enter a period of directed post-ordination formation designed to support novice deacons during the early years of ministry, particularly in the human dimension. The focus of this formation is on building proficiency in understanding and skills as a deacon in the ministries of the word, liturgy, and charity. Navigating and strengthening family life and prayer as a new deacon is also a priority. An emphasis also exists on building cultural competency in ministering to a diverse parish.

Annual formation guidelines and accountability for deacons serving in their first five years after ordination are as follows:

  • Complete the initial Ministerial Agreement with the Pastor of their assigned parish at the onset of diaconal ministry that outlines the deacon’s ministerial obligations.
    • A signed copy of this form is kept by the pastor and the deacon.
    • The initial Ministerial Agreement Form must be revised by the end of year 3, or whenever a new pastor is assigned.
  • Pastors will complete a Deacon Annual Assessment that evaluates progress on ministerial obligations at the end of the deacon’s first year in service (and years 2–5) to review with the deacon.
    • A signed copy of this form is kept by the pastor and the deacon.
  • Meet quarterly with their appointed deacon mentor couple (husband and wife) online or in person to discuss needed advice, wisdom, and support.
  • Meet quarterly online with the Continuing Education Administrator to discuss pertinent topics to support early ministry.
  • Document 24 hours over 3 years for continuing education and formation experiences that pertain directly to early ministry (e.g., Spanish learning, workshop, seminar, program, etc.).
    • See (2.2) Requirement for Experienced Deacons.
  • Meet with the Director of the Permanent Diaconate (online) at the end of the first year of post-ordination assignment (and years 2–3) to review the Annual Ministry Report, completed and submitted in advance.

Newly ordained deacons are required to attend the Annual Retreat for Deacons after the first full year of ordination. The retreat is optional during the first year of ordination.

2.2Requirement for Experienced Deacons

Deacons who have served in active service for six or more years require further education and growth in their ministry, particularly in the theological, spiritual, and pastoral dimensions. With experience in service, deacons need to learn more about the Church, scripture, theology, liturgy, and the Saints for teaching others. Experienced deacons are also ready to provide greater pastoral support in ministry with individuals and groups of parishioners.

Deacons are required to obtain twenty-four (24) hours of continuing education over a three-year period, with a minimum of six (6) hours within a single year. The three-year cycle begins on January 1 following the fifth year after ordination, or arrival into the Archdiocese of Mobile and the granting of faculties by the Archbishop.

A minimum number of hours is required across each of the three dimensions over the three-year period: Theological, Spiritual, and Pastoral. If funds are needed for any continuing education hours, deacons should seek assistance from their parish. The Office of the Permanent Diaconate is not responsible for the reimbursement of funds.

Formation guidelines over a three-year period for deacons serving six or more years in active service are as follows:

Theological Study12 hours minimum
  • This dimension includes courses from a Catholic college or institute, workshops, seminars, or conferences approved in advance by the Office of the Permanent Diaconate.
  • Those workshops, seminars, and conferences offered by the Archdiocese of Mobile or other dioceses qualify for approved continuing education hours.
  • Studies may occur online or in-person.
Spiritual Formation6 hours minimum
  • This dimension includes days of reflection/prayer, spiritual direction, and other additional retreats.
Pastoral Practice6 hours minimum
  • This dimension includes practica/certifications, personal study of books, major articles, or other material (audio/visual) that are related to one’s ministry of charity (outreach), word (evangelization/teaching), and liturgy (public prayer/devotion).

Deacons are expected to attend the Annual Retreat for Permanent Deacons, which typically takes place in the fall. A minimum of one retreat each calendar year is required by Canon Law.2 The annual retreat hours are not to be included towards the continuing education hours.

2.2.1 Accountability System

Documentation of hours will be posted on an annual basis by November 1st to a confidential web-based site for review by the Director and/or Administrator. Deacons will be notified by December 31st of each calendar year of any deficiency needed to complete the requirements.

The Continuing Education Administrator will prepare an annual report showing each deacon’s hours of continuing education. The report will be forwarded to the Director of the Permanent Diaconate, who will notify all deacons and their pastors by letter who have not fulfilled the annual and three-year hourly requirements.

The Archbishop will be notified by the Director of any deacons who do not meet these requirements and do not have an immediate plan to further meet these requirements. The Archbishop at his discretion may determine if a deacon’s faculties and/or assignment should be suspended, pending completion of the requirements.

  1. Retired deacons, deacons on pastoral leave, and deacons on leave of absence may be relieved of these requirements. ↩
  2. The cost of the annual retreat is to be paid by the deacon’s assigned parish. Deacons who cannot attend the sponsored annual retreat must plan a comparable retreat during the calendar year that is approved by the Director. The parish is not obligated to provide more than the cost of the Archdiocesan sponsored retreat. ↩
